Abstract

The Human Resource (HR) Office of Ilocos Sur Polytechnic State College (ISPSC) Tagudin Campus maintained its employee records on paper and in Excel workbooks, keeping a Personal Data Sheet for every employee, a separate form for every leave application, and two handwritten ledger cards per person, while leave approval required physically routing a printed form between three offices. This study developed NexHRIS: A Smart and Next-Generation Human Resource Information System for Ilocos Sur Polytechnic State College, a web-based system that moves this workflow online while preserving the Civil Service forms exactly as the campus uses them. The system was developed using the Laravel framework, MySQL, and Blade with Tailwind CSS, and follows the Rapid Application Development (RAD) model. NexHRIS provides employee records management, Personal Data Sheet submission and review, a role-derived leave approval chain, leave and service credit ledger cards, versioned publication of the official blank forms, HR policies with acknowledgement tracking, announcements, a digital identification card, role-based dashboards, printable management reports, and an audit trail. A distinguishing feature of the system is that official documents are produced by converting the actual uploaded workbook rather than rebuilding the form from database values, preserving the exact format of the campus's Civil Service forms. Access is governed by four roles — HR Administrator, Campus Director, Dean, and Employee — with data boundaries enforced at the query level. The acceptability of the system was evaluated using the Technology Acceptance Model (TAM) in terms of Perceived Usefulness and Perceived Ease of Use, and the Wilcoxon Signed-Rank Test was used to determine whether a significant difference existed between the existing manual process and the developed system.

Keywords: Human Resource Information System, Personal Data Sheet, Leave Management, Role-Based Access Control, Technology Acceptance Model, ISPSC

Chapter 1
Introduction

Background of the Study

The rapid advancement of technology has fundamentally transformed the way organizations manage their human resources. Across the globe, institutions are shifting away from manual, paper-based administrative processes toward integrated digital platforms that offer efficiency, accuracy, and accessibility. In government higher education institutions, Human Resource (HR) offices are primarily responsible for maintaining, organizing, and safeguarding the employment records of all personnel. These records, collectively known as the 201 files, contain an employee's complete employment history, including personal information, appointment papers, service records, certificates of eligibility, and other official employment documents. The proper management and accessibility of these records are critical to the effective administration of human resources within any institution.

Internationally, several studies confirm the urgent need for digital employee document management systems in organizations. Wahdaniah et al. (2023) demonstrated that human resource management transformation in the digital age results in more organized recordkeeping, reduced document retrieval time, and improved overall HR operational efficiency across diverse institutional settings. Similarly, Abaci and Medeni (2022) conducted a case study on the efficiency of electronic document management systems and established that digitizing employee records reduces retrieval time, minimizes physical storage requirements, and enhances organizational transparency. Orioque et al. (2024) further affirmed that an online contextualized document management system aligned with an organization's operational environment significantly enhances information access and reduces misplaced documentation. Dres and Rabut (2025) developed eDOCS, a web-based file management system, and found it to be highly effective in improving document organization, accessibility, and retrieval speed compared to traditional manual filing methods. Furthermore, Panfilova et al. (2024) noted that the integration of automated document management systems into institutional workflows facilitates diagnostic monitoring, reduces human error, and improves documentation lifecycle management, all of which are directly relevant to the employee file management needs of human resource offices in academic institutions.

In the Philippines, the digitalization of employee and institutional records has similarly gained traction among higher education institutions. Purcia and Selarde (2022) conducted a study on student registration and records management in private universities and found that the majority of institutions still rely on paper-based processes, highlighting a persistent gap in digital adoption and the urgent need for electronic records systems. Pagayonan (2022) developed an Electronic Document Management System (eDMS) for a higher education institution and found that the system improved the organization, storage, and retrieval of institutional records while reducing dependency on physical filing systems. Encarnacion et al. (2024) developed a web-based document management system for an educational office in Pagadian City that streamlined the dissemination, submission, and collection of pertinent documents, addressing challenges typically experienced by office employees in managing paper-based records. More recently, Rabulan and Glina (2025) developed a web-based file management system for the Office of National Service Training Program at Sorsogon State University, which effectively centralized student information and office records, resulting in a more secure, accurate, and faster file management process that directly reduced the burden on office personnel.

At the regional and provincial level, the need for digital records management has been consistently identified, particularly at ISPSC Tagudin Campus itself. Angala et al. (2023) developed and implemented a web-based Document Management System (DMS) at ISPSC Tagudin Campus and found that respondents were generally very satisfied with how the system was used to arrange, store, track, and manage documents within the campus, affirming that digital document systems are well-received at the institution. Bulong et al. (2025) similarly focused on ISPSC Tagudin Campus and developed DocuTrack, a web-based document management system featuring role-based access control, cloud storage integration, and an activity log system, confirming that paper-based document management remains a critical challenge for the institution as it continues to grow in terms of personnel, programs, and administrative responsibilities. Beyond ISPSC, Sebucao-Orias (2023) examined records management practices of a state university in the Philippines and identified significant deficiencies in documentation processes, while Taguiam (2024) established that non-compliance with proper records management protocols in state universities leads to operational inefficiencies, both underscoring the need for structured digital records management systems in similar institutions. The Ilocos Regional Development Plan 2023–2028 further identifies the enhancement of institutional efficiency and the adoption of digital governance tools as priority directions for all government agencies and institutions in the region (Regional Development Council, 2023). The Data Privacy Act of 2012 (Republic Act 10173) likewise mandates that all government institutions, including state colleges in Ilocos Sur, ensure the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of personal data in their custody, which directly necessitates the adoption of secure digital systems for managing sensitive employee records. Furthermore, the national directive on digital transformation articulated in the Philippine Development Plan 2023–2028 reinforces the importance of transitioning from paper-based to digital HR record management systems in institutions such as ISPSC (Department of Information and Communications Technology, 2023).

The implementation of NexHRIS at Ilocos Sur Polytechnic State College, Tagudin Campus is particularly justified by the institution's current state of HR document management. The ISPSC HR Office presently maintains all employee 201 files on paper and in Excel workbooks, keeping a Personal Data Sheet per employee, a separate form per leave application, and two handwritten ledger cards per person. Leave approval requires physically routing a printed form between three offices for signature. These manual systems are prone to document loss, physical deterioration, unauthorized access, and delays in document retrieval. As the institution continues to grow in terms of personnel and administrative responsibilities, the volume of employee records that must be managed likewise increases, making traditional paper-based processes increasingly inefficient and difficult to sustain. The absence of a centralized digital repository for employee files requires HR personnel to spend considerable time locating, reproducing, and verifying documents that could otherwise be accessed instantly through a secure electronic system. Employees, on their end, must still physically visit the HR Office just to update their Personal Data Sheet or to check their leave credits and file a leave application, adding to the workload of HR personnel who have to manually retrieve records and compute balances for every request.

A review of the systems cited above reveals a consistent limitation: all of them are document management systems, not human resource systems. The eDMS of Pagayonan (2022), eDOCS of Dres and Rabut (2025), the contextualized system of Orioque et al. (2024), the web-based system of Encarnacion et al. (2024), and the file management system of Rabulan and Glina (2025) are each designed to store, categorize, track, and retrieve files. The same is true of the two systems developed for ISPSC Tagudin Campus itself: the DMS of Angala et al. (2023) and DocuTrack by Bulong et al. (2025) both centralize institutional documents, and DocuTrack adds role-based access and activity logging, but both stop at document handling. In every one of these systems, a document is treated as a file to be uploaded and found again, and none of them process the transaction that the document represents.

NexHRIS differs from all of them in four specific ways. First, none of the reviewed systems handle the Personal Data Sheet as a governed submission with a review-and-return cycle and retained versions; they would treat a PDS as one more uploaded file. Second, none of them route a document through a multi-level approval chain in which the route is determined by the rank of the applicant, nor do they record which approvals were skipped and why. Third, none of them maintain a running-balance record such as the campus's leave and service credit ledger cards, where correcting one entry must replay every balance below it. Fourth, none of them are concerned with preserving the exact printed format of an official government form, because a document management system stores the file the user uploaded and never has to regenerate it. These are precisely the day-to-day HR transactions that ISPSC employees still complete manually, even though the campus already has a working document management system. The gap, therefore, is not the digitization of storage, which the literature has addressed thoroughly, but the digitization of the HR transactions themselves — which is what NexHRIS is designed to fill.

Recognizing this gap, NexHRIS: A Smart and Next-Generation Human Resource Information System for Ilocos Sur Polytechnic State College is conceptualized to go beyond the storage-and-retrieval model demonstrated by existing systems. Rather than covering the entire employee 201 file, the study focuses specifically on the components employees interact with most directly and repeatedly — the Personal Data Sheet, leave records, personal information, and HR policies and guidelines — allowing employees to take part in managing their own HR transactions and staying informed of institutional policies, instead of relying solely on manual, HR-mediated processes. Through the development of NexHRIS, the study seeks to address the limitations of existing manual practices, strengthen records security and accessibility, improve operational efficiency, and support compliance with government policies on digital transformation and data privacy. Ultimately, the study aims to contribute to the modernization of human resource management practices in ISPSC and provide a model for the implementation of HR-focused information systems in higher education institutions.

Conceptual Framework of the Study

The conceptual framework of this study is anchored on the Input–Process–Output (IPO) model, which provides a systematic representation of how data is transformed into meaningful outputs through the proposed system.

The Input consists of the employee 201 files, specifically the Personal Data Sheet (PDS) and leave records, together with employees' personal information and the HR policies and guidelines maintained by the Human Resource (HR) Office. These constitute the primary data required for managing employee records and facilitating HR-related transactions within the system.

The Process involves the design, development, and implementation of NexHRIS. During this stage, the system validates and updates employee information, routes leave applications through a role-derived approval chain, records approved leave against the appropriate ledger card, organizes employee data, and manages HR policies and guidelines through a centralized digital platform. These processes transform the input data into accurate, organized, and readily accessible information.

The Output consists of a centralized digital employee records repository, online Personal Data Sheet management, digital leave records and leave application management, maintained leave and service credit ledger cards, a digital employee identification record, and accessible HR policies and guidelines. Collectively, these outputs enhance the security, accessibility, and efficiency of employee records management while improving the delivery of HR services.

Overall, the IPO model illustrates how NexHRIS transforms manually managed employee records and HR information into an integrated digital system that supports efficient record management, facilitates HR transactions, and promotes effective human resource administration at Ilocos Sur Polytechnic State College.

Objectives of the Study

The main objective of this study is to develop NexHRIS: A Smart and Next-Generation Human Resource Information System for Ilocos Sur Polytechnic State College to support the secure and efficient digital management of employee Personal Data Sheets (PDS) and leave records at ISPSC Tagudin Campus. Specifically, this study aims:

  1. To analyze the current employee file management processes of the ISPSC HR Office to serve as the basis for the system design and development;
  2. To determine the system requirements for the development of NexHRIS, a web-based Human Resource Information System for the secure management of employee Personal Data Sheets, leave records, and personal information at ISPSC Tagudin Campus;
  3. To evaluate the level of acceptability of NexHRIS using the Technology Acceptance Model (TAM); and
  4. To determine whether there is a significant difference between the existing manual employee file management process and the developed NexHRIS system in terms of perceived usefulness and perceived ease of use.

Scope and Limitation of the Study

This study covers the design, development, and implementation of NexHRIS for ISPSC Tagudin Campus, focusing on four components of the employee 201 file: the Personal Data Sheet, leave records, personal information, and HR policies. The HR Administrator publishes the official Civil Service forms; employees download, fill, and upload them, and the HR Administrator reviews each submission and either approves it or returns it with remarks, retaining every version. Leave applications are routed through an approval chain determined by the rank of the applicant, since no user signs their own leave: an employee's application goes from the Dean, to the HR Administrator, to the Campus Director; a Dean's skips the Dean stage; and the Campus Director's goes to the HR Administrator alone, as no role outranks the Campus Director. Skipped stages still appear on the printed trail, marked not applicable. Once an application is fully approved, the HR Administrator records the days against either the leave ledger or the service credit ledger. The system serves four roles — HR Administrator, Campus Director, Dean, and Employee — whose data boundaries are enforced at the query level rather than in the interface, so a user cannot reach records outside their authority by modifying a web address. It also provides each employee a digital identification card with a QR code, published HR policies with acknowledgement tracking, announcements, role-based dashboards, printable management reports, and an audit trail. NexHRIS is web-based and accessible from any internet-connected device.

The study is limited in the following respects. It does not digitize the rest of the 201 file — appointment papers, service records, performance ratings, and similar documents remain in the manual custody of the HR Office. The system does not perform optical character recognition or extract data from uploaded documents, and does not verify the accuracy or authenticity of what an employee encodes, which remains the responsibility of the HR Administrator on review. Leave credits and ledger balances are encoded and maintained by the HR Administrator; the system neither computes credits from attendance or biometric data nor decides which of the two ledger cards an approved leave is recorded on. The policies module supports viewing and acknowledgement only, without a facility for employees to comment on or request revisions to a policy. The digital identification card is viewable and printable within the system and carries no RFID functionality. NexHRIS does not integrate with external government systems such as the Civil Service Commission, Government Service Insurance System, or Department of Budget and Management; is intended for ISPSC Tagudin Campus alone and does not support multi-campus deployment; has no dedicated mobile application, though it may be opened in a mobile browser; and requires an internet connection, with no offline operation.

A further limitation concerns document conversion. The fidelity of the printed output depends on what the hosting environment supports. Where a document converter is installed on the server, every official form is rendered as a PDF that reproduces the campus's own layout. On shared hosting, where no such converter may be installed, the system detects that a particular workbook cannot be reproduced faithfully — the leave form is built from drawing shapes, form controls, and an embedded document rather than from spreadsheet cells — and issues the original workbook instead of a partially rendered PDF, on the reasoning that an incomplete official form is more damaging than one opened in a spreadsheet application.

Chapter 2
Methodology

This chapter presents the methodology to be employed in the development of NexHRIS: A Smart and Next-Generation Human Resource Information System for Ilocos Sur Polytechnic State College. It discusses the research design, software development model, project timeline, project assignments, target population and locale, research instruments, and the methods of data analysis to be used in the study.

Research Design

This study will use a descriptive developmental research design. The descriptive component will involve gathering data on the current employee file management practices and challenges of the ISPSC HR Office through interviews and observations, which will serve as the basis for identifying the required system features and design specifications. The developmental component will focus on the actual design, development, and implementation of NexHRIS as a functional web-based employee file management system tailored to the needs of the HR Office and employees of ISPSC Tagudin Campus.

According to Kosie and Lew-Williams (2022), developmental research is the systematic process of designing, developing, and evaluating programs or systems to ensure internal consistency and effectiveness. The descriptive component documents each stage of development, while the developmental component emphasizes iterative enhancement and continuous refinement based on user needs and system testing results. This design framework will guide the entire research process from the initial requirements-gathering phase through system development, testing, and final evaluation.

In the context of this study, the descriptive developmental design will begin with an analysis of the current HR document management process at ISPSC Tagudin Campus, followed by the identification of the specific features required in NexHRIS based on the actual needs of the HR Administrator and employees. The system will then be designed, developed, and iteratively tested, with refinements made based on user feedback, until the final version is deployed and formally evaluated.

Software Model

This study will adopt the Rapid Application Development (RAD) Model as the software development methodology. RAD is an incremental development model that prioritizes rapid prototyping, iterative delivery, and continuous user feedback over lengthy planning and documentation phases. It is particularly well-suited for developing institutional systems where user requirements are well-defined and where the scope allows for modular development and frequent user involvement in the refinement process (Pressman, 2020).

In the context of this study, the RAD model will support the fast-paced development of NexHRIS by allowing each component — employee PDS management, the leave application and approval chain, ledger card maintenance, and personal information management — to be built and tested in stages, with feedback from the HR Administrator incorporated at each phase before proceeding to the next.

Requirements Planning

The researchers will conduct structured interviews and consultations with the ISPSC HR Administrator to identify the current challenges in employee file management and define the functional requirements of NexHRIS. These requirements will include the need for a centralized repository for PDS and leave records, a secure role-based access system, a multi-level leave filing and monitoring feature, and the faithful reproduction of the campus's official Civil Service forms.

User Design

A user-centered approach will be applied to design wireframes and interface layouts for all system modules. The HR Administrator's feedback will guide the design of the PDS review panel, the leave application review interface, the ledger card maintenance interface, and the employee account management panel, while employee user experience will be considered in the design of the PDS submission dashboard, the leave filing and monitoring interface, and the personal information view.

Construction

The system will be built using the Laravel PHP framework for the backend, Blade with Tailwind CSS and Alpine.js for the frontend interface, and MySQL as the relational database management system. PhpSpreadsheet is used to read the uploaded Civil Service workbooks, while document conversion to PDF is handled by LibreOffice where it is available and by a pure-PHP path using PhpSpreadsheet and Dompdf where it is not. Chart.js is used for dashboard visualizations. Modules will be developed sequentially, with each module tested and refined before integration with the rest of the system.

Correctness is maintained through an automated test suite that is executed after every change. At the time of writing, the suite comprises 466 automated tests covering the approval chain, the ledger balance calculations, role boundaries, document conversion fidelity, and the confidentiality of stored personnel records.

Cutover

The completed NexHRIS system will be deployed on a shared hosting environment. Development was carried out on a local server environment running PHP 8.2 and MySQL, and the deployment target is a cPanel shared hosting environment running the same versions. Uploaded personnel records are stored on the server's own private storage, outside the web-accessible directory, and are served only through the application so that every request passes the role check; only the blank official forms are served directly. The system is designed to run in either environment because the document renderer is selected from configuration, with automatic detection of what the host supports. This phase will include system testing, user orientation and training for the HR Administrator and selected employee respondents, final adjustments based on evaluation feedback, and the formal administration of the Technology Acceptance Model (TAM) questionnaire.

Population and Locale of the Study

The study will be conducted at Ilocos Sur Polytechnic State College (ISPSC), Tagudin Campus, located in Tagudin, Ilocos Sur. This institution serves as the locale of the study, as NexHRIS is specifically developed to address the employee file management needs of the ISPSC HR Office.

The respondents of this study will consist of two groups. The first group is composed of the two (2) authorized users of the NexHRIS system within the HR Office, the HR Administrator and the HR Assistant, both of whom operate the system on behalf of the HR Office. Since the total number of HR Office users is only two (2), total enumeration will be applied for this group.

The second group consists of the teaching and non-teaching personnel of ISPSC Tagudin Campus with a total population of 142 employees. The researchers used Slovin's Formula to determine the appropriate sample size for this group. From a population size of 142, a sample size of 105 employees was chosen to represent the population with a 5% margin of error.

n = N ÷ [ 1 + N(e2) ]

Where n = number of samples; N = total population; e = margin of error.

n = 142 ÷ [ 1 + 142(0.05)2 ] = 142 ÷ 1.355 = 104.79 ≈ 105

The 105 employee-respondents will be selected through simple random sampling, wherein each of the 142 employees will be given an equal chance of being selected until the required sample size is reached.

Research Instruments

The data for this study will be collected through two methods. First, structured interviews will be conducted with the ISPSC HR Administrator prior to system development to determine the current employee file management processes, challenges, and requirements that will form the basis for the NexHRIS feature design. Second, after system deployment, the Technology Acceptance Model (TAM) questionnaire will be administered to all respondents — the HR Administrator, HR Assistant, and selected employee-users — to evaluate the acceptability of the system.

The Technology Acceptance Model (TAM), originally developed by Davis (1989), is a widely validated and widely used theoretical framework for evaluating user acceptance of information systems. TAM posits that two primary constructs, Perceived Usefulness (PU) and Perceived Ease of Use (PEOU), determine a user's attitude toward using a system and, ultimately, their behavioral intention to use it. Perceived Usefulness refers to the degree to which a user believes that using the system will enhance their job performance, while Perceived Ease of Use refers to the degree to which a user believes that the system will be free of effort. The TAM questionnaire to be used in this study will assess both constructs using a five-point Likert scale, with 1 indicating strong disagreement and 5 indicating strong agreement with each statement.

The TAM instrument is particularly appropriate for this study because NexHRIS is a new system being introduced to users who have no prior experience with a digital employee file management system. Evaluating the system through TAM will allow the researchers to determine whether the users find NexHRIS useful for their HR document management tasks and whether they find it easy to operate, which are the two most critical determinants of system adoption and continued use in institutional settings.

Data Analysis

The researchers will use appropriate statistical and qualitative tools to analyze the data gathered for each objective of the study. The following presents the data analysis methods to be applied per objective.

For Objective 1, which aims to analyze the current employee file management processes of the ISPSC HR Office, the qualitative data gathered through structured pre-development interviews with the HR Administrator and HR Assistant will be analyzed using thematic analysis. This involves systematically organizing and categorizing the responses into recurring themes related to the current HR document management workflow, the challenges and inefficiencies experienced by the HR Office, and the specific system features and requirements identified by the HR personnel. The themes derived from this analysis will directly serve as the basis for the system design, feature prioritization, and functional requirements of NexHRIS.

For Objective 2, which aims to determine the system requirements for the development of NexHRIS, the functional and non-functional requirements identified during the requirements-planning interviews and consultations will be documented and organized into system specifications. These requirements will be validated with the HR Administrator and translated into the modules, user roles, and workflows of the system, ensuring that the developed system directly addresses the needs identified in Objective 1.

For Objective 3, which aims to evaluate the level of acceptability of NexHRIS based on the responses of the HR Administrator and the employees of ISPSC Tagudin Campus using the Technology Acceptance Model (TAM), the researchers will use mean and standard deviation to analyze the quantitative data gathered from the TAM questionnaire. These descriptive statistical tools will be used to determine the level of acceptability of NexHRIS as perceived by the HR Administrator, HR Assistant, and employee-users of ISPSC Tagudin Campus in terms of Perceived Usefulness (PU) and Perceived Ease of Use (PEOU). For Objective 4, which aims to determine whether there is a significant difference between the existing manual employee file management process and the developed NexHRIS system in terms of perceived usefulness and perceived ease of use, the researchers will use the Wilcoxon Signed-Rank Test. This non-parametric statistical test is appropriate for comparing two related conditions — the manual process and the NexHRIS system — using the same group of respondents, particularly when the data is ordinal in nature, as in the case of Likert-scale responses. The respondents will be asked to rate both the current manual process and the NexHRIS system using the same TAM-based items on Perceived Usefulness and Perceived Ease of Use. The Wilcoxon Signed-Rank Test will then be applied to determine whether a statistically significant difference exists between the two sets of ratings. A significance level of 0.05 will be used as the decision threshold. If the computed p-value is less than 0.05, the null hypothesis — that there is no significant difference between the manual process and NexHRIS — will be rejected, indicating that a significant difference exists in favor of the developed system.

Chapter 3
Results and Discussion

This chapter presents the findings of the study, focusing on the evaluation of the current employee management practices at the institution, the key features of the developed NexHRIS system, and the assessment of its acceptability. The results highlight how NexHRIS enhanced record security, streamlined HR transactions, and supported better accessibility and efficiency within the HR setting.

Findings

The following were the findings in relation to the interview that led to the conduct of the study:

The Current Process of Employee Records and HR Data Handling

The current HR process at ISPSC Tagudin Campus relied entirely on manual, paper-based and spreadsheet-based procedures. Employee 201 files, including the Personal Data Sheet, leave records, and other personnel documents, were kept and organized physically by the HR Office, with a Personal Data Sheet per employee, a separate form per leave application, and two handwritten ledger cards per person. Whenever an employee needed to update their Personal Data Sheet, they had to personally visit the HR Office, retrieve their physical file, and manually revise the necessary entries before having it re-filed. Leave applications followed a manual route in which a printed form was physically carried between three offices for signature, and employees were required to check their leave credits and file their requests in person, relying entirely on the HR personnel to locate their records, compute their balances, and process their applications by hand. This heavy reliance on physical filing made records vulnerable to loss, damage, and unauthorized access, while the absence of a centralized digital repository required HR personnel to spend considerable time locating, reproducing, and verifying documents for every transaction. As the volume of personnel and administrative responsibilities at the institution continued to grow, this traditional method made the process time-consuming, prone to errors, and increasingly difficult to sustain and manage efficiently.

Features of the Developed System

NexHRIS moves the campus's HR transactions online while preserving the official Civil Service forms exactly as the campus uses them. The system is presented below by module, and where a module behaves differently for each role, the corresponding interfaces are shown separately. Each plate is captioned with the interface it shows and, where useful, the role for which it is shown.

Authentication and Role-Based Access

Users sign in with an email address and password. Accounts that administer or approve on behalf of others — the HR Administrator, the Deans, and the Campus Director — are additionally challenged with a six-digit verification code sent to their registered email address, while ordinary employee accounts sign in with a password alone. Repeated failed attempts are throttled and the account is temporarily locked, and both the failure and the lockout are written to the audit trail. After sign-in, each account is taken to the interface appropriate to its role.

The four roles share one interface and differ only in what they may do. Their data boundaries are enforced in the query rather than in the interface, so a Dean who edits a web address to reach another college's records receives a refusal rather than data. The same applies to administrative functions: creating a staff account and publishing a policy are reserved to the HR Administrator, and a reviewer who reaches those addresses directly is refused, because the role field determines who signs whose leave and writing it would amount to granting authority.

Employee Accounts, Colleges, and Departments

The HR Administrator creates and maintains staff accounts, assigning each employee a college and a department, where a department belongs to exactly one college. The college an employee is assigned to determines who signs that employee's leave. Employee number, position, college, department, and role remain with the HR Administrator, while every user edits their own name, email address, contact number, photograph, and password from My Profile. Accounts are deactivated rather than deleted, so the history attached to a person survives their departure. Deans and the Campus Director may browse the staff list — a Dean sees only their own college — but cannot create or amend an account.

Publication of the Official Forms

The blank Civil Service forms are held in the system rather than circulated by hand. The HR Administrator uploads the official Personal Data Sheet and leave form, and each upload becomes a new numbered version while the previous one is retired rather than overwritten, so a submission can always be read against the version it was filled on. A published form can be previewed as a PDF from the same screen before it is issued to staff, and the original workbook remains downloadable for editing.

Leave Application and Approval Chain

An employee downloads the blank leave form, fills it in, and uploads it. The application is then routed according to the rank of the applicant, since no user signs their own leave. Each reviewer in turn reads the application as a PDF in the browser and either approves it or returns it with remarks, and a returned form restarts from the first stage. The Dean signs first for members of their own college, the HR Administrator second, and the Campus Director last; a Dean's own application skips the Dean stage, and the Campus Director's is signed by the HR Administrator alone. Each reviewer is notified by email and by the notification bell when an application reaches them. Once fully approved, the HR Administrator records the days against a ledger card and the employee may print the approval sheet.

Leave and Service Credit Ledger Cards

The campus keeps two cards per employee on the same official form. A day taken is charged either against the vacation or sick balance on the leave ledger, or against service credits on the service credit ledger, and the HR Administrator decides which card an approved leave is written on. A line on one card never appears on the other, and the leave card is kept to two decimal places while the service credit card is kept to three, as the campus's own forms require. Each line stores the balance as it stood after it, so correcting a line in the middle replays every balance below it. Monthly credits may be posted to all staff in one action, and a period already credited is skipped rather than credited twice. The HR Administrator maintains all cards; employees read their own.

Audit Trail

Every sign-in, approval, policy publication, template publication, and record change is recorded with the account and the IP address behind it. Changes to a ledger card are recorded in the same trail together with the values a line previously held, so a disputed balance can be traced to the person who altered it and to the figure it replaced. This gives the HR Administrator a reliable trail for accountability.

Data Security

To ensure data security, the system applies role-based access control that distinguishes the permissions of the HR Administrator, Campus Director, Dean, and Employee, and enforces these boundaries at the query level. Uploaded personnel records — completed Personal Data Sheets, filed leave forms, and policy attachments — are held outside the web-accessible directory and are released only through the application, so each request is checked against the role of the person making it. Only the blank official forms, which contain no personal data, are served directly. Passwords are stored hashed and must contain both letters and numerals wherever they are set. With these functions, NexHRIS enhances the security, accessibility, and efficiency of employee records management at ISPSC Tagudin Campus.
